Pick it up, pay, we bag it

Food is already made and sitting out, hot counter or chilled cabinet. A customer picks what they want, scans, and pays on their own phone. Whether staff bag it or they do is up to you.

This is not part of the $199. It would be a separate add-on, priced on its own.

How it works, start to finish

  1. They pick it up first. Packed and out on the shelf, with a price on it. They take what they want, the same as in any shop.
  2. They scan at the counter. Their own phone, no app, and they tap in what is in their hands.
  3. They pay right there. Stripe's own checkout page, the same one that takes your subscription. The shelf count drops itself from that sale, with nobody tapping it.
  4. Then it gets bagged. Either your staff check the order against their own screen and hand it over, or the customer bags it themselves and you accept losing a few percent. Your call, either way.

What this is, plainly

You choose who bags it

Have staff check each order against the counter screen and almost nothing walks, but somebody has to be standing there. Or let people bag their own and expect to lose 2% to 4% with a camera and a sign. Both are shown here, side by side, with what each one costs.

The shelf counts itself, mostly

A sale here drops the count on its own. Anything that leaves the shelf without going through this — waste, a staff snack, a mistake — still needs a hand to tap it, same as the stock tracker.

The kitchen tops it up

A fresh batch comes out and one photo fills the list in, or you tap them in by hand. Either way you tick each line as it goes on the shelf. Get low and it says so, and it says "make more," not "reorder", because nothing here is bought in.

Count at the shift change

A count resets the clock. Count weekly and a missing box could be any of seven days. Count when the shift changes and it narrows to four hours, which is a searchable amount of footage and one shift of staff.

A camera, not a lock

Micro-markets running this exact model average 2% to 4% shrinkage with a visible camera and a sign, and up to 10% with neither. That is the whole security budget: a camera and a sign.
