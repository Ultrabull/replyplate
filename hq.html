Why the add-on is $99

Square charges $99 a month for restaurant stock tracking, and about $50 a month plus $149 for a kiosk tablet. Roughly $149 a month for both, and it works. Say so if they ask.

Two catches, and they are your whole opening. It only works if they run their entire restaurant on Square. And the kiosk is a tablet to buy, charge, mind and replace.

You do both for $99, on whatever till they already have, with no hardware at all, because it runs on the customer's own phone.

Their published prices, checked August 2026. Most of them quote by phone, so treat these as estimates.

Idea: answer the phone

The phone rings at 7pm and nobody can pick it up. A missed call is a missed order, so this hits two of the four things owners actually worry about: not enough customers, and not enough staff. No hardware, set up from your laptop, and the hours and menu already come out of the ten questions.

Answer questions and take a message. Do not let it take food orders — allergies and substitutions are where it goes wrong, and a wrong order makes the customer angry at them, not at you.

This one costs you money per minute. Roughly $135 a month in raw cost for a small place, and it goes up when they get busy. Never put it in a flat price. Base plus included minutes plus a rate over, or your best client is the one you lose money on.

It must say it is an AI and that the call is recorded, in the first sentence. Several states require it. Details in the build plan.

Idea: count customers, not just food

The same camera that counts boxes can count people. Not faces, not names, just bodies and where they stop. It answers the one thing stock tracking never will: how many walked out without buying, and whether the queue caused it.

"You lost 14 customers last Friday between 12:30 and 1:10, because the queue hit eight people."

That is a more-customers problem, which beats every stock problem on the list. It also needs a camera and a small computer in each shop, which is the hardware you have avoided everywhere else. So look for a client who already has security cameras: the software can read an existing feed, and the install disappears.

Never add face recognition. Counting bodies stays outside biometric law. Matching faces between visits does not, and Illinois damages run per person. If a client asks you to spot their regulars, the answer is no.

Do not quote a price until it exists. When it does, it sits above the $99 add-on, not inside it.

Sell the first one as a pilot

It is not built yet, so do not offer it to everybody. Offer it to one restaurant, and say this:

"$300 to set it up, then $49 a month for six months, because you are my first. After that it is $99."

Their money pays for the build, and you find out what breaks in a real kitchen before anyone else is relying on it. Put the six months in writing.
