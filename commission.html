Start with the one number that matters

Independent restaurants run on three to five percent net profit. Here is what a commission does to that.

Net margin figure is the commonly cited three to five percent range for independent restaurants. The commission is DoorDash's published Plus rate. Both are estimates for comparison, not a quote.

A commission does not dent your margin on those orders. It removes it. You cook the food, you buy the ingredients, you pay the kitchen, and the order roughly breaks even.

That is fine for a stranger. The apps put you in front of people who have never heard of you, and finding new customers is worth paying for.

It is not fine for a regular. Somebody who searched your name, on Google, because they already wanted your food, and then tapped DoorDash because that was the only button there. You paid a finder's fee to be found by somebody who had already found you.

The number that decides it is deliveries per hour

The part that can end a business

Your driver's own car insurance almost certainly excludes delivery. Personal policies do. And your general liability policy excludes cars.

So if your driver hits somebody on the way to a drop, their insurer denies it, your liability policy denies it, and you are liable, because they were working for you when it happened.

The cover for this is called hired and non-owned auto, roughly $150 a month. If you hire drivers and skip it, one crash costs more than every commission you would ever have paid.

And using the staff you already have is worse than it sounds

Sending a server out for twenty five minutes does not cost you their wage. It costs you the tables they were not serving. On a Friday that is the most expensive delivery you will ever make.

The option most restaurants have never been shown

Take the order yourself. Pay a flat fee for the driver only.

DoorDash and Uber both sell the driver on their own, without the marketplace. The order comes in through your website, the customer sees your name and not theirs, and you pay roughly $7 a delivery. No commission, no hiring, no scheduling, no insurance of your own.

The reason it wins is simple. A commission grows with the order. A flat fee does not.
