Their ordering link, for Google Maps

Google lets a restaurant put its own link in the "Order online" list on its listing, free, marked Preferred by business above DoorDash and the rest. This is the link to give them. It opens straight on their menu.

Then: Business Profile → Food ordering → Add a link → paste → turn on preferred → Save. Before they discount it, read the note in the setup guide: Grubhub's contract still asks for the same prices everywhere and DoorDash ranks you lower when yours are cheaper. A free side beats a price cut.

Website chat helper

Answers the questions a restaurant gets asked all day. There is no AI in the widget itself — it can only repeat answers the owner confirmed, so it can never guess at an allergy question. Sell it as a website add-on. See the demo.

1. Everything the owner told you

Opening hours, parking, dietary options, bookings, kids, dogs, wifi, payment, delivery. Rough notes are fine.

2. Pickup orders (optional)

Customers tap items, then send the finished order from their own phone. No money changes hands here and no order sits in a dashboard nobody opens. Leave the menu blank to skip this.

Loyalty card (optional)

Order N times, get something free. Counted on the diner's own phone, and the claim is written into the order text so the owner sees it before they give anything away. Leave the number at 0 to switch it off.

Tonight's offer (optional)

Shows in the chat only on the days you tick, and takes itself down on the stop date. Their problem is rarely Saturday. Leave the text blank to switch it off.

Always set a stop date. An offer nobody took down is how a restaurant ends up advertising Christmas in March.

Diners pay at the counter. If a restaurant wants an order paid up front they text their own payment link back, which is question 7 on the Clients tab. Nothing about paying is ever built into the chat.

What to look for (60 seconds on their Google listing)
  1. Star rating and review count. 3.8 to 4.4 with plenty of reviews is the sweet spot. They care, and there's room.
  2. Scroll the recent reviews. How many have a reply from the owner? That's your opening.
  3. Find the newest reply. "March" or "never" is the line that lands.
  4. Copy one review, ideally a bad one. You'll send them a reply to it, written for free.
  5. Check their Instagram or Facebook. When was the last post?
