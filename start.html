Question 7

Diners tap what they want and text you the order from their own phone. No card machine, no commission, and normally they pay at your counter. The last part of this question is about the times you would rather have the money first.

Google lets you put your own ordering link on your listing, above DoorDash and the rest. A small thank you for using it is what makes people switch, and you keep the commission either way. Give something that is not a price cut if you can. Grubhub's contract asks you to keep prices the same everywhere and DoorDash quietly ranks you lower when yours are cheaper. A free side has none of that trouble and pulls just as hard.

Normally they pay at your counter, and most places leave it that way. When you would rather have the money before you start cooking, you reply to their order text with your own payment link. Nothing switches on and nothing is automatic: you decide order by order, which is the point. A twelve dollar pizza does not need it. A party order for twenty might. It costs you something each time you use it. Your card provider takes roughly 3%, so about $1.20 on a $40 order, and a card payment can be disputed in a way cash at the counter cannot. What it buys you is no more food cooked for somebody who never turns up. The money goes straight to you either way, into your own account. It never comes near us.
