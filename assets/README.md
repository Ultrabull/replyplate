# The Olive Table: every picture, and the prompt that makes it

One file. Thirteen pictures and one piece of music. Each prompt below is finished and ready to paste as
it is, with the house style already inside it, so nothing has to be assembled.

Drop a finished file into this folder under the exact name given and it takes
over its slot the next time the page loads. Nothing else to change, no code to
touch, and you can do them one at a time. Every slot has a drawing behind it,
so a missing file is never a hole in the page.

---

## Before you start: four things that decide whether this works

**1. Put the subject in the middle of the frame.**
The gallery tiles are square on a phone, so only the centre of a wide picture
is ever seen. A beautiful shot with the pizza off to one side will show a wall.
This matters more than the prompt.

**2. Keep every shot in the same light.**
One warm light source, deep shadows, the same dark wooden surfaces. Thirteen
pictures that agree with each other look expensive. Thirteen good pictures that
disagree look like a stock photo folder.

**3. Watch the file size.**
Photos under about 400KB, videos under about 3MB. There is no server behind
this site, so a heavy file is felt immediately on a phone. If a photo comes out
at 4MB, run it through any "compress JPEG" tool before you upload it.

**4. No faces, no text, no logos.**
Hands and forearms are fine and make food pictures better. Faces date a site
and raise questions about who they are. Every prompt below already says so.

**5. Check the bottom right corner for a sparkle.**
Google's free Gemini stamps a small four-pointed star into the corner of every
picture and every clip it makes. It is faint, but on a dark photo you can see
it, and it was on all thirteen of these.

It has been painted out of all of them. Not cropped: cropping it off would have
cost a tenth of every frame and pushed the centred subject off centre, which is
rule 1 above. The star is the same shape every time, so it was measured once
and then, in each picture, the pixels it covered were replaced with a piece of
the same picture from just beside it.

If you generate a new one, expect the star again. Google AI Studio gives you
the file without it, which saves the bother.

## 1. `hero.jpg` — full screen, behind the restaurant name

```
A wood-fired brick pizza oven glowing orange in a dark, empty restaurant,
photographed head on from a few steps back. Flames visible inside the mouth of
the oven. A long wooden peel leaning against the wall beside it. Warm light
pooling on the stone floor in front, everything at the edges falling away into
shadow. The middle of the frame is calm and uncluttered so text can sit across
it. Shot on a 50mm lens at f/2, warm tungsten and firelight, deep shadows, dark
wood and blackened steel, moody and appetising, photorealistic. No text, no
watermark, no logos, no faces. Wide landscape, 3:2.
```

## 14. `room.mp3` — the room sound

**This one is in.** The speaker button in the demo header plays it. It is 28
seconds, 438KB, and it repeats without a seam. The generated sound is still
there behind it, for any browser that will not play the file.

**What was done to the file you sent.** Google's music tool hands back an mp4,
which is a video file with the music inside it. The video was 2MB of the 2.8MB
and nobody would ever have seen it, so the music was lifted out on its own.
Then the loop was made: the clip was 30.8 seconds and ended on a quieter note
than it started, so it would have bumped every time it went round. It is now
cut to a whole number of bars and the last second and a half is faded over the
first, which is the trick section 14 describes below. Nothing was re-recorded.

**If you ever replace it, keep it 60 to 90 seconds, under 3MB, and make sure it
loops.** It plays on a quiet website, so nobody should ever be able to tell
where it restarts.

```
Warm, sparse instrumental for an empty Italian restaurant in the late
afternoon, an hour before service. Nylon string guitar played very softly,
brushed upright bass, a little room reverb, no drums and no beat. Slow, warm
and unhurried. It should sit under a conversation rather than lead one.
Nothing dramatic, no build, no melody a person would hum. Loopable, seamless,
90 seconds. No vocals, no lyrics, no percussion, no sudden dynamic changes.
Instrumental only.
```

**Three things this prompt does on purpose:**

- **No vocals.** Words compete with the words on the page. Somebody trying to
  read a menu while a voice sings at them will turn it off.
- **No beat.** A drum makes a person aware of time passing. That is the last
  thing a page you want them to linger on should do.
- **No melody worth humming.** Ambience that is too good stops being ambience.
  If they notice the tune, they have stopped reading.

### Getting it to loop

Most AI music tools do not loop cleanly. Generate about 90 seconds, then pick a
stretch that starts and ends on the same quiet texture. If the join still
clicks, cross-fade the last two seconds over the first two in any free audio
editor. Play it round three times before you upload it, because a seam you can
hear is worse than no music at all.

## Uploading them

1. Put the file in this `assets` folder with the exact name from the table.
2. Commit and push to `main`.
3. Wait about two minutes, then open the demo and hard refresh.

If a picture does not appear, the name is wrong. It is almost always the name:
`gallery1.jpg` instead of `gallery-1.jpg`, or `hero.JPG` instead of `hero.jpg`.
Lowercase, exact, including the dash.

## Checking them once they are in

Open the demo on your phone and scroll the whole way to the bottom. Two things
to look for:

**Anything cut off.** If a pizza has lost half of itself in a square tile,
that photo needs recropping with the subject further into the middle. It is the
picture that is wrong, not the site.

**Anything that jars.** One photo shot in bright daylight, among twelve shot in
firelight, will stand out more than a missing photo would. Better to leave that
slot on its drawing and try again than to have one picture arguing with the
rest.

## One thing to be clear about with paying clients

AI pictures are fine here. The Olive Table is not a real place and the page
says so at the top.

For a restaurant that is paying you, use real photographs, either yours or
theirs. A generated picture of food they do not actually serve, on a website
with their name on it, is a promise that breaks the moment the plate lands on
the table, and it is their reputation that pays for it, not yours. A phone
camera near a window at eleven in the morning beats a perfect picture of the
wrong dinner.

If their own pictures are the weak part, that is the paid shoot. You book a
photographer in their town, brief them on the six shots the Photos tab in the
console lists, and hand over the edited set. The price is in `config.js` as
`photoPrice`. Get the photographer's quote before you quote the restaurant,
because that number is what you are marking up and it moves a lot by city.
