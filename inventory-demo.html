Stock, in both places

Your stock lives in two places: the kitchen, and wherever you keep the rest. One helper taps as it arrives, as it goes to the kitchen, and as it gets used. You see both piles from your phone.

This is not part of the $199. It would be a separate add-on, priced on its own.
The question this answers

The kitchen is nearly out of rice. Do you buy more, or is there a sack sitting at the unit already? You cannot walk past a storage unit the way you walk past your own walk-in. Get it wrong and you either waste a trip or order twice.

How it works, in three buttons

  1. A delivery arrives. Ten sacks of rice land at the unit. Your helper taps "+1 bag" ten times, or once per sack as it comes off the van.
  2. Some goes to the kitchen. They carry two sacks over and tap the arrow twice. The unit goes down by two, the kitchen goes up by two. Nothing is lost between them.
  3. The kitchen uses it. A cook opens a sack, someone taps "−1 bag." That is the one tap that needs a habit, and it is one person's job, not everybody's.
  4. You get told which problem you have. Low in the kitchen but fine at the unit means send someone. Low in both means order. Two different sentences, so you never guess.

What this is, plainly

One person, not everybody

This only works because the helper who moves stock is the one who taps. Asking a cook mid-service to log a pound of chicken never survives a busy Friday. Asking the person already carrying the box is a different question.

Send someone, or order

Two low states, two different sentences. Anything that just says "low" makes you go and look, which is the work you were trying to avoid.

Count each place on its own

You can only count what you are standing in front of. So the kitchen and the unit are counted separately, and each keeps its own clock for when it was last checked.

Only as true as the last tap

Miss a tap and the number drifts until someone counts. That is what the count is for, and the gap it finds is exactly the stock that moved without anyone saying so.

A nudge, not an order

When something crosses the line you set, you are told, and told the amount to bring it back. Nothing is ordered for you. You still make the call.

Your places, your amounts

A garage, a second unit, a cousin's cold room, whatever you actually use. You choose what goes on the list and what counts as low, for each place.
