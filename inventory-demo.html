Stock, without the counting

A code by the walk-in. Tap it when something is taken, or when a delivery comes in. The kitchen below is set up the way yours would be, with your own ingredients.

This is not part of the $199. It would be a separate add-on, priced on its own.

How it works, in four taps

  1. Something comes in. A sack of basmati rice arrives. Someone taps "+3kg," once.
  2. Something goes out. A cook takes chicken for tonight's orders. They tap "−2kg."
  3. Nobody counts the shelf. No walking round the kitchen with a clipboard. The number just adds itself up from those taps, all day.
  4. A nudge before it runs out. When something gets low, you are told, with the amount to reorder.

What staff do, back of house

A drawing of the code, not a real one you can scan. On the wall, this is one sticker by the walk-in fridge. Tapping the buttons below does what scanning it would.

What this is, plainly

A nudge, not an order

When something crosses the line you set, you are told, and told the amount to bring it back. Nothing is ordered for you. You still make the call.

Only as true as the last tap

If somebody takes stock without tapping, the number is wrong until the next one. That is on the habit in the kitchen, not on the code.

Your ingredients, your amounts

Rice, paneer, ghee, whatever is actually in your kitchen. You choose what goes on the list, and what counts as low, for each one.
